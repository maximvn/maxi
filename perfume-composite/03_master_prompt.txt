STEP 2 — REBUILD THE SET.
Seamless bordeaux paper sweep, no horizon line, no wall-to-floor seam. STEP 3 — THE SIX BOTTLES.
PRODUCT LOCK — THE HIGHEST-PRIORITY RULE HERE. Every bottle is reproduced exactly as it appears in its own reference photograph. You are photographing six real objects in a new place; you are not designing them. Do not redesign, reproportion, recolour, relabel or embellish any part of any bottle. No new cap shape. No added collar, ring, band, engraving, foil, seal, ribbon, cord or tag. No changed plaque shape, size, position, border, typography, letter-spacing or layout, and no added or removed words. The only things that change from the reference are the light falling on the bottle and the angle it is seen from. Wherever anything below disagrees with a reference, the reference wins.
THE ROSTER — EXACTLY SIX, ONE OF EACH. Six bottles in frame, no more and no fewer: @image1 once, @image2 once, @image3 once, @image4 once, @image5 once, @image6 once. One reference in, one bottle out, one to one. Never repeat, clone, mirror or duplicate a bottle. Never invent a seventh one. No two bottles in the frame share a liquid colour or a label. Before finishing, count them: six bodies, six caps, six plaques, six different names.
IDENTICAL VESSELS. All six are units of one bottle from the same mould: identical height, diameter, glass thickness, base puck, cap, brim disc, neck collar, shoulder ring and plaque size. Not one is taller, shorter, wider or narrower than another. Only the colour of glass and liquid and the wording on the plaque change. Any apparent size difference comes purely from lens distance and stays under about eight percent — the long lens compresses them into a matched set.
FILL LEVEL. Every bottle is full. The liquid rises to the same height in all six, high in the shoulder just below the black collar, so the six meniscus lines form one consistent level. No bottle is half full, part used or low; no oversized air gap.
Use the following only to check your render against the references, never to invent from: a right cylinder of heavy clear glass, flat shoulder, straight sidewall, flat base, no taper; a thick solid glass puck in the bottom fifth; a four-part piano-black closure — flat-topped cap, wide flat brim disc, short neck collar, broader shoulder ring; one square inset plaque dead centre on the sidewall. No spray trigger, no visible atomiser, no plastic seam, no box.
Liquid colours, read from the references: @image3 pale champagne #EADCB6 densifying to #C9B98A; @image4 fractionally creamier, #E8DBC3 to #C6B69C; @image2 olive-chartreuse #B9C36A to #7E8A3C, throwing a green transmitted pool; @image1 glacier water #A9D3E8 to #6FA9C6, the only cool note in the frame; @image5 amber-cognac #C08A46 to #7A4A18, glowing like whisky when the edge light passes through; @image6 deepest-tinted, smoked but still transparent, #4A4B3C toward #6E7052 where the key rakes it, its tone-on-tone dark olive plaque debossed in the plaque colour and legible only by relief — do not brighten it or turn it silver.
Discard the original lighting of all six references. Carry over geometry, proportion, colour and label artwork only, then re-light every bottle from scratch inside the bordeaux set.

COMPOSITION — ALL SIX BOTTLES STANDING.
Every bottle stands upright on the books, base flat and in full contact. Nothing floats, nothing is suspended, nothing is tilted, no bottle leans on another. The two offset stacks give three standing heights, and the six are staggered across them so the group reads as a rising diagonal from lower-left to upper-right, not a straight shop-shelf line — a playful, hand-arranged rhythm in which no two bottles sit at the same angle.
Back tier, top book of the main stack: @image6 slightly left of centre, rotated about eight degrees so its label faces camera-left of dead-on, and @image5 behind and right, turned twelve degrees the other way, its cap the highest point of the group.
Middle tier, second book down: @image3 forward and right of @image6, label square to camera, @image2 to its left, turned about fifteen degrees away.
Front tier, lowest, secondary stack at right: @image4 nearest the lens, label square on, and @image1 at the right edge, turned about twenty degrees away, partly overlapped by the book in front.
Depth comes from the staggered heights and the overlaps — never from levitation. Never let two silhouettes tangent-touch or two cap discs exactly overlap. Leave breathing room above the tallest cap and a clean bordeaux margin on all four edges. All six bottles fully inside the frame, all six labels readable — none turned away, mirrored or cropped.

CAMERA.
Medium format digital capture, Hasselblad H6D-100c with an HC Macro 120mm f/4 II — a compressed, near-orthographic short tele that keeps verticals vertical and renders the cylinders honestly. Aperture f/11, ISO 50, 1/125s, single frame. Focus plane on the label of @image3, depth of field carrying all six bottles and the book stack into critical sharpness while the bordeaux paper falls gently soft, never breaking into bokeh discs.
Camera height level with the middle of the main stack — a straight-on product elevation, not a hero low angle, not a flat-lay. Lens axis tilted down one to four degrees. Subject distance about 1.4 metres. Perspective flat and product-honest: no converging verticals, no wide-angle stretch, no keystone.

LIGHTING — FOUR SOURCES.
One, KEY: a 120 by 180 centimetre softbox, double-diffused plus a quarter grid cloth, upper front-left at forty-five degrees azimuth, forty degrees elevation, 1.5 metres out, feathered so the hot centre falls just behind the objects. 5400K. It sets every shadow direction and the specular streak down each cap.
Two, FILL: a one by one metre white bounce card camera-right at 0.8 metres, 1.7 stops under the key, opening the right-hand shadow faces without flattening the modelling. Key-to-fill four to one.
Three, EDGE / RIM: a 30 by 120 centimetre strip box behind and left at 130 degrees azimuth, forty-degree grid, a third of a stop over the key but tightly contained. This lights the bottles from within, driving the bright edge highlight down the left flank of each cylinder and igniting the juice in all six.
Four, BACKGROUND WASH: a small fresnel, twenty-degree grid, quarter CTO, high centre on the paper, creating the luminance pool and the corner falloff. It never spills onto the products.
No fifth source, no on-axis ring, no coloured gels, no practical light, no sunlight, no hard kicker other than the strip box.

SHADOWS.
All shadows travel down and to the right, consistent with the single key. Every bottle is standing, so every bottle carries a hard contact shadow: a tight dense dark line exactly where the glass base meets the book board, roughly ninety-two percent opacity at the contact and decaying fast, with no gap between base and shadow. If a base and its shadow do not meet, the shot has failed. No detached or floating shadows anywhere in the frame. Cast shadows are soft-edged, penumbra about a third the height of the bottle throwing them, and warm — carrying red-magenta from the bordeaux bounce, never neutral grey and never blue — falling to about twenty-five percent density within one and a half bottle heights.
Deep ambient occlusion in the gaps between books and in the page-block striations. These bottles do not cast flat opaque shadows — they cast partially transmitted shadows with a coloured caustic core: champagne under @image3 and @image4, green under @image2, blue under @image1, amber under @image5. @image6 casts the densest, with a thin bright caustic escaping its base puck.

COLOUR, HUE AND BALANCE.
White balance 5200K, tint plus four toward magenta. A deep bordeaux ground carrying olive, chartreuse, amber, mustard, cream, kraft and champagne, with one small glacier-blue accent. Reds deep and dense rather than vivid — richness from density, not saturation. Black point lifted very slightly: the darkest corners read deep maroon-black, not pure zero. Highlight rolloff gentle, nothing clipped except pinpoint glints. Contrast a soft S with a print-editorial shoulder. Full colour — not black and white, duotone, split-toned or sepia — but a near-monochrome ground so the six product colours do the chromatic work.

TRANSPARENT GLASS — THE CRITICAL NOTE.
These are real glass bottles and must read as glass, never as coloured plastic cylinders pasted onto a background. All six are see-through. Through the body of each you must actually see the scene behind it — the ivory page-block striations, the book covers, the bordeaux paper — carried through the liquid, tinted its colour, laterally compressed and vertically flipped by the curve of the cylinder. That inverted, tinted, distorted slice of background inside the glass is the strongest proof of transparency; without it the bottles look fake. @image3, @image4 and @image1 are clearest. @image2 and @image5 are more saturated, so the background behind them reads diffused and colour-cast. @image6 carries the deepest tint but still transmits — light passes through its edges and base puck and a softened suggestion of the page-block shows through the thinner upper section. Nothing is opaque; no bottle is a flat block of colour.

GLASS AND LIQUID PHYSICS.
Render real optics, not painted glass. The left and right vertical edges of each cylinder read as dense dark bands from total internal reflection; the centre reads brighter and more transparent. The base puck collects the key and throws a bright caustic pool just in front of the bottle. The glossy black closures carry a long soft specular streak curving down their sides and a sharp mirrored highlight on the flat brim edge. The hammered silver plaques on @image1 and @image2 break the highlight into dimpled orange-peel micro-speculars.

REALISM AND FINISH.
Commercial catalogue grade — this must read as a captured frame, not a render. Very fine luminance-only grain at about two percent, medium-format-film in character. No motion blur, gaussian or radial blur, tilt-shift, glow bloom, lens flare, light leaks, bokeh balls, dust overlays, vintage filter, HDR halo, plastic CGI sheen or wet-look over-render. Geometry undistorted: no barrel, pincushion or fisheye. Chromatic aberration essentially absent. Sharpening moderate.
