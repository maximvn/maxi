STEP 2 — REBUILD THE SET.
Seamless bordeaux paper sweep, no horizon line, no wall-to-floor seam. Backdrop luminance pools softly behind and just above the cluster at roughly #5C2830, settles to #4A1D24 across the mid-field, and falls away about 1.6 stops into #2C1015 at the edges and #1E0A0E in the corners. Cover cloth in forest green, cream, kraft tan, burnt orange, faded ochre and aged olive, worn at the corners. No dust jackets, barcodes, spine titles or legible lettering on any book.

STEP 3 — THE SIX BOTTLES.
All six share one industrial-design language and it must not drift. A perfect right cylinder of heavy optically clear glass: flat top shoulder, straight unbroken sidewall, flat base, no taper, no waist. The bottom eighteen to twenty-two percent of the body is a solid glass puck that behaves like a lens. The closure is a four-part stack in high-gloss piano black — a flat-topped cylinder cap on a wide flat brim disc about 1.7 times the cap diameter with a razor-thin edge, on a short neck collar, on a broader shoulder ring whose lower edge crosses the glass as a crisp horizontal line. A square inset label plaque with a thin raised border sits dead centre on the sidewall at about fifty-five percent of the bottle width, curving slightly with the cylinder, set in a geometric grotesque, all caps, wide letter-spacing. Glass body height to diameter about 1.35 to 1; total height with closure about 1.55 times the body height. No spray trigger, no visible atomiser, no plastic seam, no batch code, no box.
Take each bottle's liquid colour, glass tint, plaque finish and label wording verbatim from its own reference. @image3 and @image4 are fully transparent — background detail must be visible through them, refracted and inverted; @image3 runs pale champagne #EADCB6 at the top densifying to #C9B98A through the longest light path, @image4 a fractionally creamier #E8DBC3 to #C6B69C. @image2 is translucent olive-chartreuse, #B9C36A at the fill line down to #7E8A3C in the deep section, throwing a green-tinted transmitted pool onto the surface. @image1 reads as tinted glacier water, #A9D3E8 up top to #6FA9C6 at the puck — the only cool note permitted in the frame, staying small and controlled against the bordeaux. @image5 is warm amber-cognac, #C08A46 in the thin upper section deepening to #7A4A18 at the base, glowing like whisky when the edge light passes through it. @image6 is smoked and semi-opaque: you sense a lit core but cannot see through it, only a rim of light penetrating the edges and base, #4A4B3C reading toward #6E7052 where the key rakes it, with a tone-on-tone dark olive plaque whose lettering is debossed in the plaque colour and legible only by relief — do not brighten it, do not turn it silver.

CAMERA.
Medium format digital capture, Hasselblad H6D-100c with an HC Macro 120mm f/4 II — a compressed, near-orthographic short tele on this format, keeping verticals vertical and rendering the cylinders honestly. Aperture f/11, ISO 50, 1/125s, single frame, no focus-stacking artefacts. Focus plane on the label of @image3, depth of field carrying all six bottles and the full book stack into critical sharpness while the bordeaux paper behind falls gently soft, never breaking into bokeh discs.
Camera height level with the middle of the main book stack — a straight-on product elevation, not a hero low angle, not a flat-lay. Lens axis tilted down one to four degrees, no more. Subject distance about 1.4 metres. Perspective flat and product-honest: no converging verticals, no wide-angle stretch on the nearest bottles, no keystone.

LIGHTING — FOUR SOURCES.
One, KEY: a 120 by 180 centimetre softbox, double-diffused plus a quarter grid cloth, upper front-left at about forty-five degrees azimuth and forty degrees elevation, roughly 1.5 metres out, feathered so the hot centre falls just behind the objects. 5400K, neutral-warm. It builds every shadow direction and the long specular streak down each glossy black cap.
Two, FILL: a one by one metre white bounce card camera-right at about 0.8 metres, 1.7 stops under the key. It opens the right-hand shadow faces without flattening the modelling. Key-to-fill ratio roughly four to one.
Three, EDGE / RIM: a 30 by 120 centimetre strip box behind and left at about 130 degrees azimuth, with a forty-degree egg-crate grid, running a third of a stop over the key but tightly contained. This lights the bottles from within: it drives the bright edge highlight down the left flank of each cylinder, ignites the juice in @image1 through @image5, and gives @image6 its penetrating rim without opening up its smoked body.
Four, BACKGROUND WASH: a small fresnel with a twenty-degree grid and quarter CTO aimed high centre on the paper, creating the luminance pool behind the cluster and the falloff into the corners. It never spills onto the products.
No fifth source, no on-axis ring, no coloured gels, no practical light in shot, no sunlight or window light, no hard kicker other than the strip box.

SHADOWS.
All shadows travel down and to the right, consistent with the single key. Under every grounded bottle sits a tight, dense contact shadow at the exact line where glass meets book board, roughly ninety-two percent opacity at the contact and decaying fast. Cast shadows are soft-edged, penumbra about a third the height of the object throwing them, and warm in tone — carrying red-magenta from the bordeaux bounce, never neutral grey and never blue — falling to about twenty-five percent density within one and a half bottle heights.
The three suspended bottles cast detached shadows on the books and paper beneath: larger than the object, markedly softer, lower in opacity than a contact shadow. This detachment is the strongest cue that sells the levitation and must be present and correct for all three.
Deep ambient occlusion in the gaps between books and in the page-block striations. Transparent bottles do not cast flat opaque shadows — they cast partially transmitted shadows with a coloured caustic core: warm champagne under @image3 and @image4, green under @image2, cool blue under @image1, burnt amber under @image5. @image6, being smoked, casts a near-solid shadow with only a thin bright caustic escaping its base puck.

COLOUR, HUE AND BALANCE.
White balance 5200K, tint plus four toward magenta. A deep bordeaux ground carrying olive, chartreuse, amber, mustard, cream, kraft and champagne, with one small controlled glacier-blue accent. Reds are deep and dense rather than vivid — richness from density, not saturation. Saturation restrained and editorial. Black point lifted very slightly for a filmic sit: the darkest corners read deep maroon-black, not pure zero. Highlight rolloff gentle and long, nothing clipped except pinpoint glints on the black caps, silver plaques and glass edges. Contrast curve a soft S with a print-editorial shoulder. This is a full-colour photograph — not black and white, not duotone, not split-toned, not sepia — but it holds a near-monochrome ground so the six product colours do all the chromatic work.

GLASS AND LIQUID PHYSICS.
Render real optics, not painted glass. The left and right vertical edges of each cylinder read as dense saturated dark bands from total internal reflection; the centre reads brighter and more transparent. The thick base puck collects the key and throws a bright caustic pool just in front of the bottle. Where the juice is transparent, the page-block edges and book covers behind must be visible through it — refracted, laterally compressed and vertically inverted, as a real glass cylinder distorts what is behind it. A crisp meniscus sits high in the shoulder, with a separate optical zone in the air gap above it. The glossy black closures carry a long soft specular streak from the softbox curving down their sides and a sharp mirrored highlight on the flat brim edge. The hammered silver plaques on @image1 and @image2 break the highlight into fine dimpled orange-peel micro-speculars, not one smooth reflection.
