STEP 2 — REBUILD THE SET.
Seamless bordeaux paper sweep, no horizon line, no wall-to-floor seam. Backdrop luminance pools behind and above the cluster at roughly #5C2830, settles to #4A1D24 mid-field, and falls about 1.6 stops into #2C1015 at the edges and #1E0A0E in the corners. The paper is completely matte — no sheen, no mirrored bottles in it.
Two offset stacks of closed hardcover books form a stepped plinth — a taller four-to-five volume main stack lower left and centre, a shorter two-to-three volume stack set back right. Page-blocks face camera, so the ivory leaf edges, #EFE6D6 to #DCCDB4, read as fine horizontal striation with micro-shadow between the leaves. Each book is rotated four to twelve degrees against the one below — hand-stacked, never machine-aligned. Cover cloth in forest green, cream, kraft tan, burnt orange, faded ochre and aged olive. No dust jackets, barcodes, spine titles or legible lettering.

STEP 3 — THE SIX BOTTLES.
IDENTICAL VESSELS — NON-NEGOTIABLE. Six units of one bottle, from the same mould: identical height, diameter, glass thickness, base puck, cap, brim disc, neck collar, shoulder ring and plaque size. Not one is taller, shorter, wider or narrower than another. Only the colour of glass and liquid and the wording on the plaque change. Any apparent size difference comes purely from distance to the lens and stays under about eight percent — the long lens compresses them, so they read as a matched set.
FILL LEVEL. Every bottle is full. The liquid rises to the same height in all six, high in the shoulder just below the black collar, so the six meniscus lines form one consistent level. No bottle is half full, part used or low; no oversized air gap.
A perfect right cylinder of heavy optically clear glass: flat top shoulder, straight unbroken sidewall, flat base, no taper, no waist. The bottom eighteen to twenty-two percent is a solid glass puck that behaves like a lens. The closure is a four-part stack in high-gloss piano black — a flat-topped cap on a wide flat brim disc about 1.7 times the cap diameter with a razor-thin edge, on a short neck collar, on a broader shoulder ring whose lower edge crosses the glass as a crisp horizontal line. A square inset label plaque with a thin raised border sits dead centre on the sidewall at about fifty-five percent of the bottle width, curving slightly with the cylinder, in a geometric grotesque, all caps, wide letter-spacing. Body height to diameter about 1.35 to 1; total height with closure about 1.55 times body height. No spray trigger, no visible atomiser, no plastic seam, no box.
Take each bottle's liquid colour, glass tint, plaque finish and label wording from its reference. @image3 runs pale champagne #EADCB6 at the top densifying to #C9B98A through the longest light path; @image4 is fractionally creamier, #E8DBC3 to #C6B69C. @image2 is translucent olive-chartreuse, #B9C36A at the fill line down to #7E8A3C in the deep section, throwing a green transmitted pool onto the surface. @image1 reads as tinted glacier water, #A9D3E8 up top to #6FA9C6 at the puck — the only cool note in the frame. @image5 is warm amber-cognac, #C08A46 in the thin upper section deepening to #7A4A18 at the base, glowing like whisky when the edge light passes through. @image6 is the deepest-tinted, smoked but still transparent, a lit core inside and light penetrating the edges and base, #4A4B3C toward #6E7052 where the key rakes it, with a tone-on-tone dark olive plaque whose lettering is debossed in the plaque colour, legible only by relief — do not brighten it or turn it silver.
Discard the original lighting of all six references. Carry over geometry, proportion, colour and label artwork only, then re-light every bottle from scratch inside the bordeaux set.

COMPOSITION — ALL SIX BOTTLES STANDING.
Every bottle stands upright on the books, base flat and fully in contact. Nothing floats, nothing is suspended, nothing is tilted, no bottle leans on another. The two offset stacks give three standing heights, and the six are staggered across them so the group reads as a rising diagonal from lower-left to upper-right rather than a straight shop-shelf line — a playful, hand-arranged rhythm in which no two bottles sit at the same angle.
Back tier, top book of the main stack: @image6 slightly left of centre, rotated about eight degrees so its label faces camera-left of dead-on, and @image5 behind and right, turned twelve degrees the other way, its cap the highest point of the group.
Middle tier, second book down: @image3 forward and right of @image6, label square to camera, @image2 to its left, turned about fifteen degrees away.
Front tier, lowest, secondary stack at right: @image4 nearest the lens, label square on, and @image1 at the right edge, turned about twenty degrees away, partly overlapped by the book in front.
Depth comes from the staggered heights and the overlaps — never from levitation. Never let two silhouettes tangent-touch or two cap discs exactly overlap. Leave breathing room above the tallest cap and a clean bordeaux margin on all four edges. All six bottles fully inside the frame, all six labels readable — none turned away, mirrored or cropped.

CAMERA.
Medium format digital capture, Hasselblad H6D-100c with an HC Macro 120mm f/4 II — a compressed, near-orthographic short tele that keeps verticals vertical and renders the cylinders honestly. Aperture f/11, ISO 50, 1/125s, single frame. Focus plane on the label of @image3, depth of field carrying all six bottles and the book stack into critical sharpness while the bordeaux paper falls gently soft, never breaking into bokeh discs.
Camera height level with the middle of the main stack — a straight-on product elevation, not a hero low angle, not a flat-lay. Lens axis tilted down one to four degrees. Subject distance about 1.4 metres. Perspective flat and product-honest: no converging verticals, no wide-angle stretch, no keystone.

LIGHTING — FOUR SOURCES.
One, KEY: a 120 by 180 centimetre softbox, double-diffused plus a quarter grid cloth, upper front-left at forty-five degrees azimuth, forty degrees elevation, 1.5 metres out, feathered so the hot centre falls just behind the objects. 5400K. It sets every shadow direction and the specular streak down each cap.
Two, FILL: a one by one metre white bounce card camera-right at 0.8 metres, 1.7 stops under the key, opening the right-hand shadow faces without flattening the modelling. Key-to-fill four to one.
Three, EDGE / RIM: a 30 by 120 centimetre strip box behind and left at 130 degrees azimuth, forty-degree grid, a third of a stop over the key but tightly contained. This lights the bottles from within, driving the bright edge highlight down the left flank of each cylinder and igniting the juice in all six.
Four, BACKGROUND WASH: a small fresnel, twenty-degree grid, quarter CTO, high centre on the paper, creating the luminance pool and the corner falloff. It never spills onto the products.
No fifth source, no on-axis ring, no coloured gels, no practical light, no sunlight, no hard kicker other than the strip box.

SHADOWS.
All shadows travel down and to the right, consistent with the single key. Every bottle is standing, so every bottle carries a hard contact shadow: a tight dense dark line exactly where the glass base meets the book board, roughly ninety-two percent opacity at the contact and decaying fast, with no gap between base and shadow. If a base and its shadow do not meet, the shot has failed. No detached or floating shadows anywhere in the frame. Cast shadows are soft-edged, penumbra about a third the height of the bottle throwing them, and warm — carrying red-magenta from the bordeaux bounce, never neutral grey and never blue — falling to about twenty-five percent density within one and a half bottle heights.
Deep ambient occlusion in the gaps between books and in the page-block striations. These bottles do not cast flat opaque shadows — they cast partially transmitted shadows with a coloured caustic core: warm champagne under @image3 and @image4, green under @image2, cool blue under @image1, burnt amber under @image5. @image6 casts the densest shadow, with a thin bright caustic escaping its base puck.

COLOUR, HUE AND BALANCE.
White balance 5200K, tint plus four toward magenta. A deep bordeaux ground carrying olive, chartreuse, amber, mustard, cream, kraft and champagne, with one small glacier-blue accent. Reds deep and dense rather than vivid — richness from density, not saturation. Black point lifted very slightly: the darkest corners read deep maroon-black, not pure zero. Highlight rolloff gentle, nothing clipped except pinpoint glints on caps, plaques and glass edges. Contrast a soft S with a print-editorial shoulder. Full colour — not black and white, duotone, split-toned or sepia — but a near-monochrome ground so the six product colours do all the chromatic work.

TRANSPARENT GLASS — THE CRITICAL NOTE.
These are real glass bottles and must read as glass, never as coloured plastic cylinders pasted onto a background. All six are see-through. Through the body of each you must actually see the scene behind it — the ivory page-block striations, the book covers, the bordeaux paper — carried through the liquid, tinted its colour, laterally compressed and vertically flipped by the curve of the cylinder. That inverted, tinted, distorted slice of background inside the glass is the strongest proof of transparency; without it the bottles look fake. @image3, @image4 and @image1 are clearest — the background reads almost legibly through them. @image2 and @image5 are more saturated, so the background behind them reads diffused and colour-cast. @image6 carries the deepest tint, but it is still glass and still transmits — light passes through its edges and base puck, its core glows where the rim light strikes it, and a softened suggestion of the page-block shows through the thinner upper section. Nothing is opaque. No bottle is a flat block of colour.

GLASS AND LIQUID PHYSICS.
Render real optics, not painted glass. The left and right vertical edges of each cylinder read as dense dark bands from total internal reflection; the centre reads brighter and more transparent. The thick base puck collects the key and throws a bright caustic pool just in front of the bottle. The glossy black closures carry a long soft specular streak curving down their sides and a sharp mirrored highlight on the flat brim edge. The hammered silver plaques on @image1 and @image2 break the highlight into dimpled orange-peel micro-speculars, not one smooth reflection.

REALISM AND FINISH.
Commercial catalogue grade — this must read as a captured frame, not a render. Very fine luminance-only grain at about two percent, medium-format-film in character. No motion blur, gaussian or radial blur, tilt-shift, glow bloom, lens flare, light leaks, bokeh balls, dust overlays, vintage filter, HDR halo, over-sharpened edges, plastic CGI sheen or wet-look over-render. Geometry undistorted: no barrel, pincushion or fisheye. Chromatic aberration essentially absent. Sharpening moderate.
