TRANSPARENT GLASS, OPAQUE MARK. The glass is real and see-through: whatever stands behind the bottle is visible through the body, tinted deep green, laterally compressed and vertically flipped by the curve, and broken along the facet edges into slightly offset panels. The green runs light and luminous where the glass is thin and dense, almost black-green through the deepest section and the thick base, which acts as a lens. WHAT THE GLASS REFLECTS. Glass transmits what is behind it and mirrors what is in front of it at the same time; a render that only transmits looks fake. The front surface carries mirrored highlights of this scene's own light, stretched and bent by the taper, with the darker parts of the surroundings reading as deep vertical bands down the flanks. Facet edges catch thin bright specular lines. The white ribbed cap picks up light in the grooves so the fluting reads as alternating light and shade; the gold collar returns a warm metallic band, bright along its top edge, darker underneath.

PHYSICAL EVIDENCE. A flawless object reads as computer-generated. Include, subtly: a speck or two of dust catching the light, a faint barely-there fingerprint low on the glass, a hair-fine mould seam or one tiny bubble deep in the base, the smallest wear on a cap edge. Nothing dirty — just evidence of a real object that has been handled.

FABRIC PHYSICS — GET THIS RIGHT. Any cloth in this picture hangs BEHIND the bottle and to the sides. Nothing is draped in front of it. If any fabric were to pass between the lens and the bottle, then the bottle behind it would have to be genuinely obscured — softened, desaturated, its edges lost, its printed mark unreadable — because you would be looking through a woven screen. A sharp, saturated, fully legible bottle seen through a veil is physically impossible and is the fastest way to make the picture look fake. So: keep all cloth behind the bottle, never across it.

NOT AI-LOOKING. This is a photograph made by a person with a camera. Avoid the tells: perfect symmetry, a subject dead centre, everything equally sharp, surfaces too clean, speculars too uniform, colour spread too evenly, lighting with no dominant direction. Commit to one light, let large areas go dark or empty, let the composition sit off balance, let something be cut by the frame.

There is no beach, no shore, no water, no ground plane and no horizon line anywhere in this picture. THE COMPOSITION.
The camera angle is nearly straight down, almost overhead, looking at the sand and seaweed as if photographed from directly above. The bottle is tilted at roughly 45 degrees, lying diagonally across the frame from upper-left to lower-right, with its body appearing to rest (optically) on the rippled sand surface. Strands of dark kelp cross around and behind it, creating diagonal lines that echo and contrast with the linear ripple patterns. The composition is deliberately asymmetrical: the densest seaweed cluster occupies the left-centre, with open sand visible in the right side and lower portion.

ART DIRECTION.
Soft overhead light from directly above or slightly front, revealing the sand ripples with gentle dimensional modeling without harsh shadows. The light is warm-toned, creating the sense of coastal golden-hour or overcast daytime. The ripple patterns are the hero of the composition — the seaweed and bottle interrupt and interact with those linear forms. The overhead camera angle emphasizes the flatness and texture of the sand while the bottle's tilt and the seaweed's organic curves create visual interest.

LIGHT. A single overhead or near-overhead light source, soft enough that the ripple patterns read with gentle definition but not harsh contrast. The light skims across the sand troughs, revealing their linear pattern. The seaweed catches warm-toned highlights along its edges. The bottle, lying on its side in the composition, catches the light along its upturned flank with a soft specular line, its downturned side reading in shadow. The transmission through the glass shows the warm sand tones beneath it tinted green. The overall effect is bright, warm, and textural rather than dramatically shadowed.

COLOUR AND GROUND. The ground is the rippled sand itself — pale cream-beige with subtle blue-grey shadows in the ripple troughs, creating linear rhythm across the frame. The seaweed is deep golden-brown and rust, warm against the cool sand tones. The void behind and around is a warm-neutral pale tan that fades to deeper warm-grey in the periphery, roughly 1.5 stops of subtle falloff. The emerald glass reads as a cool jewel-tone jewel against the warm, earthy coastal palette. Warm 6000K, moderate saturation, blacks minimal (the whole image reads bright and aerial).
