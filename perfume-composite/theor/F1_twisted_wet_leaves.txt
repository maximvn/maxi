TRANSPARENT GLASS, OPAQUE MARK. The glass is real and see-through: whatever stands behind the bottle is visible through the body, tinted deep green, laterally compressed and vertically flipped by the curve, and broken along the facet edges into slightly offset panels. The green runs light and luminous where the glass is thin and dense, almost black-green through the deepest section and the thick base, which acts as a lens. The monogram and the words BRUME ORPIN and THEOR are pale opaque ink on the outer surface — you do not see the background through the letterforms, and they do not refract or invert with what passes behind.

WHAT THE GLASS REFLECTS. Glass transmits what is behind it and mirrors what is in front of it at the same time; a render that only transmits looks fake. The front surface carries mirrored highlights of this scene's own light, stretched and bent by the taper, with the darker parts of the surroundings reading as deep vertical bands down the flanks. Facet edges catch thin bright specular lines. The white ribbed cap picks up light in the grooves so the fluting reads as alternating light and shade; the gold collar returns a warm metallic band, bright along its top edge, darker underneath.

PHYSICAL EVIDENCE. A flawless object reads as computer-generated. Include, subtly: a speck or two of dust catching the light, a faint barely-there fingerprint low on the glass, a hair-fine mould seam or one tiny bubble deep in the base, the smallest wear on a cap edge. Nothing dirty — just evidence of a real object that has been handled.

FABRIC PHYSICS — GET THIS RIGHT. Any cloth in this picture hangs BEHIND the bottle and to the sides. Nothing is draped in front of it. If any fabric were to pass between the lens and the bottle, then the bottle behind it would have to be genuinely obscured — softened, desaturated, its edges lost, its printed mark unreadable — because you would be looking through a woven screen. A sharp, saturated, fully legible bottle seen through a veil is physically impossible and is the fastest way to make the picture look fake. So: keep all cloth behind the bottle, never across it.

NOT AI-LOOKING. This is a photograph made by a person with a camera. Avoid the tells: perfect symmetry, a subject dead centre, everything equally sharp, surfaces too clean, speculars too uniform, colour spread too evenly, lighting with no dominant direction. Commit to one light, let large areas go dark or empty, let the composition sit off balance, let something be cut by the frame.

THE COMPOSITION.
The bottle is tilted at roughly 40 degrees, its cap end raised, resting (in appearance) against one of the major twisted leaf formations. The leaves spiral around it — one major knot behind and above, one reaching down and to the left, creating a natural cradle. The composition is off-balance: the dense tangle of leaves occupies the left-centre, with a significant pocket of empty void in the lower right for breathing room. The bottle interrupts the composition without dominating it.

ART DIRECTION.
Strong raking light from upper left front, skimming across the leaf surfaces at a very shallow angle. This light reveals the papery texture, the moisture on the leaves, every twist and compression. The far side of the leaves falls into deep shadow. The bottle catches the same light along its upturned flank, with a thin bright specular edge. The whole image is tilted no more than 15 degrees off vertical, a subtle cant suggesting the moment the arrangement has been caught.

LIGHT. One single raking source from upper left, nearly horizontal, creating extreme dimensionality. The lit edges of the twisted leaves read luminous and crisp, almost glowing where the light catches moisture. The valleys between the twisted forms fall into rich, deep shadow — almost black, creating powerful form and depth. The bottle's edge catches a bright line, the far side reading in deep green shadow. The contact between bottle and leaf glows with transmitted light through the glass.

COLOUR AND GROUND. The ground is a warm-neutral pale grey that darkens significantly outward: lighter grey-green near the leaves (reading almost as void), shifting through mid-tone grey, into deep charcoal and near-black in the periphery and especially in the deep shadow valleys, a gradient of roughly 2.5 to 3 stops. The palette is cool-green: sage, moss, deep forest green, with touches of grey-green. The emerald glass reads warm and jewel-like against the cooler leaf tones. Neutral 5500K, high contrast between lit edges and shadow, blacks very deep.

FINISH.
A real photograph, not a render. Fine luminance-only grain around three percent, heavier in the shadow areas. Allow a real lens its small dishonesties: a pronounced bloom on the brightest specular highlights from the wet leaf edges, minimal fringing, slight corner falloff. Focus plane on the printed mark and the nearest twisted leaf cluster; the far leaves falling noticeably soft. Depth of field separation is crucial to selling the floating sensation.
