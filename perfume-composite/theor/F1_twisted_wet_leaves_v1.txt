TRANSPARENT GLASS RENDERING — THE DUAL NATURE OF GLASS.
Glass is neither fully transparent nor fully reflective — it is simultaneously both. A photorealistic render shows transmission and reflection at once. The body of the bottle reveals what lies behind it, tinted deep emerald, the image laterally compressed and vertically inverted by the curved glass surface, fractured into panels along the facet edges. Where the glass is thin, the green reads light and luminous; where the glass is thick (the deepest section, the dense base acting as a lens), the green runs almost black-green, deep and absorbing. The monogram and wordmark BRUME ORPIN and THEOR are pale opaque ink on the outer surface — you do not see background through these letterforms; they do not refract or invert. The front surface simultaneously carries mirrored reflections of this scene's own light. The reflected highlights are stretched and bent by the bottle's taper, appearing as thin bright vertical bands down the flanks. The white cap with fine grooves catches light in the fluting, reading as alternating light and shade, each ridge catching, each valley falling into shadow. The gold collar reflects a warm metallic band, bright along its top edge where light strikes directly, darker and richer underneath.

THE SCENE INSPIRED BY REFERENCE — TWISTED WET LEAVES.
The reference photograph shows organic forms twisted, knotted, compressed into sculptural shapes. The leaves are long, glossy, wet. Their surfaces catch raking light revealing every fiber, every vein, creating brilliant specular highlights along the twisted edges. The wetness is luminous — not water droplets, but a sheen of moisture that makes the leaf surfaces glow where light catches them. Deep shadows fall into the valleys and folds between the twisted forms, creating a sense of organic tension and compression. The color palette is cool green — sage, moss, deep forest, with subtle grey-green tones. The background in the reference is pale muted, reading almost as void, not as an environment. The entire arrangement feels suspended in darkness, with light skimming across it from one dominant direction, creating extreme dimensionality.

TRANSLATION — BOTTLE AS HERO AMONG LEAVES.
Draw the visual language, mood, atmosphere, and lighting direction from the reference, but compose it around the bottle as the central protagonist. The twisted leaves no longer fill the frame; they serve as a sculptural frame for the bottle. The bottle is tilted at a dynamic 35 to 40 degrees, its cap end raised, the base angled down and away. One or two major leaf formations cradle the bottle — not obscuring it, but creating a natural nest around it. The leaves spiral and weave around the bottle, their twisted forms echoing its taper. Some leaves cross behind the bottle; some reach in front, but crucially, no leaf passes between the lens and the bottle's body — any leaf that appeared to cross the front would have to completely obscure the bottle behind it, which defeats the purpose of showing the product. Instead, leaves frame, suggest context, provide depth layers. The bottle's transparency shows the leaf texture through its glass body. The bottle is sharp and crystal clear; the leaves further back fall into soft focus, creating depth separation.

COMPOSITION — ASYMMETRICAL HIERARCHY.
The bottle occupies the dominant visual real estate — it is not dead-center, but it is clearly the focal point. Perhaps slightly left-of-center or positioned to catch the strongest light. The twisted leaves are positioned asymmetrically around it: a dense knot of leaves might occupy one quadrant, creating visual weight and balance through asymmetry, not through symmetry. The right side of the frame or the lower portion remains open, with empty void, breathing room. The composition is deliberately off-balance, with the weight of the bottle and its surrounding leaf nest creating a visual center of gravity, but the overall frame is not perfectly symmetrical. This feels like a caught moment, not a composed still life.

LIGHT — SINGLE RAKING SOURCE, EXTREME DIMENSIONALITY.
One single light source, raking across from the upper left at a very shallow angle — nearly horizontal. This light is the hero light; it reveals form through extreme contrast. The lit edges of the twisted leaves read luminous, almost glowing where the light catches the moisture. The moisture on the leaf surfaces catches specular highlights that seem almost to emit light. The valleys and folds between the twisted leaves fall into rich, deep shadow — almost black, creating powerful form and depth through chiaroscuro. The bottle's near edge (the side facing the light) catches this same raking light along its upturned flank, creating a thin bright specular line that runs up the glass. The far side of the bottle reads in deep green shadow. The printed mark on the bottle catches gentle light, remains legible, catches a subtle highlight. The contact point between the bottle and a leaf near it glows with transmitted light through the emerald glass, a warm green glow where the light passes through and is colored by the glass.

The palette is dominated by cool greens: sage, moss, deep forest green. The leaves read in these tones. The background ground is a warm-neutral pale grey, reading almost as void. PHOTOREALISTIC FINISH — NOT A RENDER.
This is a photograph made by a person with a camera, not a 3D render. Include photographic imperfections and realistic details: fine luminance-only grain around 3 percent, heavier in the shadow areas. Allow a real lens its small dishonesties: a pronounced bloom (halo) on the brightest specular highlights from the wet leaf edges catching light, minimal chromatic fringing (tiny color separation) along the high-contrast edges, a subtle corner falloff where the image darkens slightly toward the edges. No perfect sharpness everywhere — focus is selective. The focus plane lands on the printed mark on the bottle and the nearest twisted leaf cluster; the far leaves fall into noticeable soft focus. The depth-of-field separation created by the raking light angle and close focus plane is crucial to selling the three-dimensional, floating sensation of the composition.

DEPTH OF FIELD — SELECTIVE FOCUS FOR DIMENSIONALITY.
The near field (bottle and closest leaves) reads sharply. The mid-field (the major leaf formations around the bottle) reads with full detail. The far field (leaves receding into the background) falls progressively softer, creating a clear depth gradient. This separation is not extreme, but it is noticeable and intentional. The bokeh (background blur) is soft and natural, not rendered perfectly smooth.

COMPOSITION CANT — SUBTLE DUTCH TILT.
The entire image is tilted subtly, no more than 10 to 15 degrees off vertical. This slight cant suggests the moment the arrangement has been caught, as if the photographer tilted the camera or the arrangement has shifted slightly. This is not a symmetrical, rigidly composed image; it feels candid, a moment in time.

AVOID AI TELLS — PHOTOGRAPHIC AUTHENTICITY.
Avoid: perfect symmetry, the subject dead-center, everything equally sharp and equally detailed, surfaces too clean and pristine, speculars too uniform and perfect, color spread too evenly, lighting with no dominant direction. Commit to one light source. Let large areas go dark or empty. Let the composition sit off-balance. Let something be cut by the frame edge. Let textures read as natural and imperfect. Let the bottle's printed mark show subtle variation in ink coverage. The overall image must read as a real photograph, not a procedurally generated render.

PHYSICAL EVIDENCE — REALITY MARKERS.
Include subtle evidence that this is a real object that has been handled: a speck or two of dust catching the light on the lower body of the bottle, a faint barely-there fingerprint (nearly invisible) on the glass somewhere on the mid-body, a hair-fine mould seam somewhere deep in the base (visible only in shadow), one tiny bubble deep in the base glass (catching a highlight if light reaches it), the smallest wear on an edge of the white cap (a tiny flat spot or burnish mark). Nothing dirty or damaged — just evidence of a real object that has been photographed and handled by real hands. These details should be subtle; they are not the point of the image, but they prevent the photograph from reading as computer-generated.

The leaves are organic plant material, not fabric. They can weave and cross freely without the physics constraints of woven cloth. However, maintain the principle: no leaves completely obscure the bottle from the lens. The bottle remains visible and recognizable in all its details.
