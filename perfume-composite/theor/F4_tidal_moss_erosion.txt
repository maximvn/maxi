TRANSPARENT GLASS, OPAQUE MARK. The glass is real and see-through: whatever stands behind the bottle is visible through the body, tinted deep green, laterally compressed and vertically flipped by the curve, and broken along the facet edges into slightly offset panels. The green runs light and luminous where the glass is thin and dense, almost black-green through the deepest section and the thick base, which acts as a lens. The monogram and the words BRUME ORPIN and THEOR are pale opaque ink on the outer surface — you do not see the background through the letterforms, and they do not refract or invert with what passes behind.

WHAT THE GLASS REFLECTS. Glass transmits what is behind it and mirrors what is in front of it at the same time; a render that only transmits looks fake. The front surface carries mirrored highlights of this scene's own light, stretched and bent by the taper, with the darker parts of the surroundings reading as deep vertical bands down the flanks. Facet edges catch thin bright specular lines. The white ribbed cap picks up light in the grooves so the fluting reads as alternating light and shade; the gold collar returns a warm metallic band, bright along its top edge, darker underneath.

PHYSICAL EVIDENCE. A flawless object reads as computer-generated. Include, subtly: a speck or two of dust catching the light, a faint barely-there fingerprint low on the glass, a hair-fine mould seam or one tiny bubble deep in the base, the smallest wear on a cap edge. Nothing dirty — just evidence of a real object that has been handled.

FABRIC PHYSICS — GET THIS RIGHT. Any cloth in this picture hangs BEHIND the bottle and to the sides. Nothing is draped in front of it. If any fabric were to pass between the lens and the bottle, then the bottle behind it would have to be genuinely obscured — softened, desaturated, its edges lost, its printed mark unreadable — because you would be looking through a woven screen. A sharp, saturated, fully legible bottle seen through a veil is physically impossible and is the fastest way to make the picture look fake. So: keep all cloth behind the bottle, never across it.

NOT AI-LOOKING. This is a photograph made by a person with a camera. Avoid the tells: perfect symmetry, a subject dead centre, everything equally sharp, surfaces too clean, speculars too uniform, colour spread too evenly, lighting with no dominant direction. Commit to one light, let large areas go dark or empty, let the composition sit off balance, let something be cut by the frame.

THE COMPOSITION.
The camera is positioned close and slightly angled down, creating an intimate view of the eroded sand surface. The bottle is tilted at roughly 30 degrees and nestled (optically) among the moss-covered rocks, as if settled into the tidal landscape. Dark green moss-rocks are positioned asymmetrically around it: one major rock cluster behind and to the left, another smaller one to the right, with pale weathered sand filling the negative spaces. The composition is deliberately off-centre: the bottle and rocks occupy the left-centre, leaving a significant area of pale eroded sand on the right for balance.

ART DIRECTION.
Overcast or soft diffused light, creating gentle definition on the sand texture without harsh shadows. The light is cool-toned, emphasizing the weathered, timeless quality of the erosion patterns. The close perspective means the viewer is intimate with the surface texture — every grain, every ripple, every erosion line is visible. The cool palette and soft light create a contemplative, quiet mood. No dramatic angles or cant — the composition reads as a straight, intimate close-up of a natural tidal landscape.

LIGHT. A single soft, diffused light source from slightly front and above, almost a cool overcast light. This light gently models the sand ripples and erosion patterns, revealing their form through subtle tonal variation rather than harsh contrast. The moss-rocks read with gentle definition, their surface texture visible. The bottle catches soft highlights along its edges, the transmission through the glass showing the cool sand tones beneath it tinted green. The overall effect is subtle, cool, and textural — the light is a servant to the texture, not the star of the show.

COLOUR AND GROUND. The ground is the pale, weathered sand itself — whites, pale blues, grey-whites, cool in tone. The moss-rocks are deep forest green, almost black-green in the shadow areas, creating warm visual counterpoint to the cool sand. The void around is a cool pale grey that fades to subtle cooler grey in the periphery, roughly 1 stop of soft falloff. The entire image reads in cool tones except for the deep green of the moss. Neutral 5400K or cooler, moderate to high contrast between the pale sand and the deep green rocks, blacks subtle and open. The emerald glass reads jewel-like and warm against the cool palette.

FINISH.
A real photograph, not a render. Fine luminance-only grain around two and a half percent, visible throughout the mid-tones and shadow areas. The close perspective creates noticeable depth-of-field separation, with the immediate foreground reading sharpest and the background noticeably softer.
