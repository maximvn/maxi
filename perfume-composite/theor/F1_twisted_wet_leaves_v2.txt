The bottle's glass is a complex optical element. It is transparent — whatever stands behind and around the bottle is visible through the glass body, but this view is transformed. The transmission shows the colors of whatever is behind and around the bottle, tinted deep emerald green by the glass itself. Where the glass is thin (the upper shoulder, the thin areas of the facet panels), the transmitted light reads as bright, luminous, nearly translucent green. Where the glass is thick (the deepest part of the body, the dense base which acts as a thick lens), the transmitted green reads as very dark, almost black-green, dense and absorbing light. The front surface of the bottle simultaneously acts as a mirror, reflecting the light in this scene. The reflected highlights are stretched and bent by the bottle's conical taper, appearing as thin bright vertical bands running down the flanks. The facet edges catch additional thin bright specular lines where the light reflects off the sharp edges of the glass. The white ribbed cap reflects light in the grooves — each vertical fluting channel catches light along the ridge, and each valley between the flutings falls into shadow, creating a visual rhythm of light and dark that reads as the texture and detail of the cap. The gold collar reflects a warm metallic band; the top edge where light strikes most directly reads bright and shiny; the underside where light reaches from a glancing angle reads richer and darker, deep gold. The monogram and wordmark are pale opaque ink, not transparent; light does not pass through these letterforms. They do not refract or invert with the background behind them. They read as flat opaque marks against the transparent glass. This dual nature — transparency and reflection, transmission and mirror — is essential to rendering the bottle as real and photorealistic.
