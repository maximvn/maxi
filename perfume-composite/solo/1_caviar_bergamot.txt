CAMERA.
Medium format digital capture, Hasselblad H6D-100c with an HC Macro 120mm f/4 II, tethered. Aperture f/9, ISO 50, 1/125s, single frame. Subject distance about 0.8 metres, which keeps the cylinder honest and the verticals vertical. Focus plane on the label plaque. LIGHTING — FOUR SOURCES.
One, KEY: a 120 by 180 centimetre softbox, double-diffused, upper front-left at forty-five degrees azimuth, forty degrees elevation, 1.2 metres out, feathered so the hot centre falls just behind the bottle. 5400K. This is the source the glass mirrors.
Two, FILL: a one metre white bounce card camera-right, about two stops under the key — enough to keep the shadow side of the glass alive without lifting the darkness of the ground.
Three, EDGE / RIM: a 30 by 120 centimetre strip box behind and left at 130 degrees, gridded, a third of a stop over the key but tightly contained. This lights the bottle from within and ignites the liquid so the colour glows rather than sits flat.
Four, BACKGROUND: a small fresnel, twenty-degree grid, quarter CTO, aimed at one spot behind and above the bottle to burn the warm pool into the wall and let everything around it fall away. It never spills onto the product.
No fifth source, no on-axis ring, no coloured gels, no practical light in shot, no sunlight or window light, no hard kicker other than the strip box.

The bottle does not cast a flat opaque shadow — it casts a partially transmitted shadow with a cool blue caustic core escaping the base puck.

TRANSPARENT GLASS, OPAQUE LABEL — TWO RULES THAT MUST BOTH HOLD.
First, THE GLASS IS TRANSPARENT. The cylinder wall and the liquid inside are see-through: you look through the bottle and out the other side. It is translucent-to-transparent and reads as tinted water — background detail comes through it clearly. Through the glass above, below and to either side of the label you must see what stands behind the bottle — the cream fore-edges, the cover boards, the burgundy wall — carried through the liquid, tinted its colour, laterally compressed and vertically flipped by the curve of the cylinder. That inverted, tinted slice of background inside the glass is the proof of transparency. The fill line reads as a meniscus with clear glass and air above it, and the base is a solid lens of thick glass. Nothing about the glass is opaque and it is never a flat block of colour.
Second, THE LABEL IS NOT TRANSPARENT. The square plaque is a solid physical sticker or inset metal plate on the glass — not glass, not a window, not a tinted panel. You cannot see the liquid, the far wall, the books or the backdrop through it. It blocks the view completely and its tone stays constant whatever stands behind the bottle. It sits proud of the glass, catching its own thin highlight along the raised edge and dropping a hairline shadow below. Where the plaque is, the bottle reads solid.

WHAT THE GLASS REFLECTS — THE HALF THAT IS USUALLY MISSING.
Glass does two things at once and both must be visible. It transmits what is behind it, and it mirrors what is in front of it. A render that only transmits looks fake, and that is the single most common failure in this shot.
The front surface of the cylinder mirrors the studio. The softbox reads as a soft-edged bright vertical band down the left flank, brightest near the shoulder and fading toward the base, its edge smeared by the curve. Camera-right, the dark unlit side of the room mirrors as a deep vertical band reading almost black against the liquid. Low on the bottle the cream page-edges beneath bounce up and smear across the glass as a warm horizontal glow. These reflections sit ON the glass, in front of the liquid colour and in front of anything seen through the bottle, and they are what tell the eye the object stands in a room.
The glossy black cap mirrors the softbox as a distinct soft-edged rectangle of light on its curved face, the dark surround wrapping either side of it, and one hard bright line along the flat brim edge. The neck collar and shoulder ring catch narrower versions of the same.
You also see the far inside wall of the bottle through the near wall: the inner surface of the back of the cylinder reads as a second, dimmer edge inside the silhouette. The liquid surface at the fill line reads as a shallow ellipse rather than a straight line, because the lens looks very slightly down onto it, and the glass thickness at the shoulder catches a bright ring.

PHYSICAL EVIDENCE — THE IMPERFECTIONS THAT PROVE IT IS REAL.
A flawless object reads as computer-generated. Include, subtly: one or two specks of dust on the shoulder or the board catching the key; a faint, barely-there fingerprint low on the glass where a hand set it down; a hair-fine mould seam or one tiny bubble deep in the base; slight unevenness in the meniscus; the smallest wear on a cap edge. Nothing dirty, nothing distracting — just evidence of a physical object that has been handled and set on a table. The books are imperfect the same way: a bent leaf, a dog-eared corner, uneven foxing.

GLASS EDGES AND THE BASE.
The left and right vertical edges of the cylinder go dense and dark from total internal reflection while the centre stays brighter and more transparent. The thick base puck gathers the key and throws a bright caustic pool onto the board just in front of the bottle. The hammered silver plaque breaks the key into fine dimpled orange-peel micro-speculars rather than one smooth reflection, and at this magnification that pebbled metal texture must be visible.

REALISM AND FINISH.
This was photographed on a table, not built in software. Fine luminance-only grain at about three percent, medium-format-film in character and heavier in the shadows. Allow the small optical honesty of a real lens: a whisper of bloom around the brightest speculars, a trace of fringing at extreme highlight edges, very slight corner falloff. What to avoid is the CG look — surfaces too clean, edges too perfect, speculars too uniform, everything equally sharp, colour too evenly distributed. No motion blur, tilt-shift, lens flare, light leaks, bokeh balls, dust overlays, vintage filter or HDR halo. Sharpening moderate, never crunchy.
